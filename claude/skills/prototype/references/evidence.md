# Evidence for Prototyping

Use this reference when choosing prototype type, fidelity, or evidence limits.

## Applied principles

1. Prototype one important uncertainty rather than an underspecified product.
2. Choose the cheapest medium that can answer the question.
3. Match fidelity to the uncertainty: requirements, interaction, technical feasibility, scale, or integration behavior.
4. Separate experimental code from production delivery and record limits on generalization.

A mapping study and multi-case study developed an empirical model of software prototyping and found prototypes are used to communicate requirements and reduce ambiguity, incompleteness, inconsistency, and feasibility risk:

- [An empirically based model of software prototyping](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-023-10331-w)

An experimental field study of more than 200 projects found that requirements uncertainty generally needs several complementary techniques rather than one isolated practice:

- [Requirements uncertainty](https://doi.org/10.1145/1062455.1062554)

Case evidence on paper prototyping shows different modes reveal different requirement types, supporting a question-specific choice of medium:

- [Loud and Interactive Paper Prototyping](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07662)
