# Evidence for Technical Research

Use this reference for broad evidence synthesis, conflicting studies, or claims that influence high-cost decisions.

## Applied principles

1. State the research question, scope, applicability criteria, and stopping condition before searching widely.
2. Prefer sources that directly govern the user's version and environment.
3. Record disconfirming evidence and distinguish source authority from source count.
4. Separate evidence collection from inference and recommendation.
5. Make the method auditable enough to reproduce material conclusions.

Kitchenham and Charters organize systematic software-engineering reviews into planning, conducting, and reporting, with an explicit protocol intended to reduce bias:

- [Guidelines for performing Systematic Literature Reviews in Software Engineering](https://madeyski.e-informatyka.pl/download/Kitchenham07.pdf)

Guidance on updating software-engineering reviews reinforces date boundaries and repeatable search strategies:

- [Guidelines for the Search Strategy to Update Systematic Literature Reviews](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05542)
- [When to Update Systematic Literature Reviews](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.06183)

Routine product research does not need the ceremony of a systematic review. Apply only the controls needed to answer the decision faithfully.
