# Source Quality

Select sources according to the claim they must support. Search ranking, popularity, repetition, and polished presentation do not establish authority.

## Match the source to the claim

1. Use normative standards, specifications, or official product documentation for required behavior, syntax, compatibility, and conformance.
2. Use official release notes, lifecycle pages, package registries, and maintained repositories for versions, release dates, deprecations, and support status.
3. Use vendor advisories and the responsible vulnerability authority for security status. Use third-party databases to corroborate or discover records, not to override the controlling advisory without evidence.
4. Use source code, tests, and maintainer decisions for implementation behavior when documentation is silent. Treat repository issues as evidence that a report exists, not as proof of supported behavior or prevalence.
5. Use original papers, datasets, and published methodology for research claims. Check population, sample, measurement, limitations, and whether later work supersedes the result.
6. Use reproducible benchmarks with disclosed hardware, software versions, configuration, workload, warm-up, and measurement method for performance claims. Do not compare results collected under materially different conditions.
7. Use dated registry statistics or methodologically described surveys for adoption claims. State the measured population and avoid generalizing beyond it.
8. Use independent technical analyses and community reports to find failure modes and operational experience. Corroborate material conclusions with primary evidence when possible.

## Reconcile evidence

1. Prefer the source that controls the claim within the exact product, version, jurisdiction, or standard scope.
2. Prefer direct evidence over summaries and current applicable evidence over older evidence, unless historical behavior is the question.
3. Check whether conflicting sources use different definitions, editions, configurations, or dates.
4. Report a material unresolved conflict with both sources and its effect on the decision.

## Preserve traceability

For each consequential claim, retain the source owner, direct URL, publication or update date when available, applicable version or scope, and whether the conclusion is direct or inferred. Cite the underlying page or artifact, never a search-result snippet. Record access dates only when the content is undated or expected to change without versioned history.
