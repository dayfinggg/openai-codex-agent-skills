# Research conversations

- Ask about a specific recent event, existing workflow, workaround, cost, frequency, and consequence rather than hypothetical future behavior.
- Do not pitch the solution before learning about the problem. Compliments and feature opinions are weaker evidence than past actions and commitments.
- Ask neutral questions and allow silence. Do not lead the participant toward the answer the team hopes to hear.
- Separate facts, interpretations, assumptions, and opportunities in research notes.
- Treat willingness to spend time, money, access, reputation, or operational effort as stronger evidence than enthusiasm alone.
- Protect consent, privacy, dignity, and sensitive data throughout recruitment, sessions, storage, analysis, and reporting.
