# Sketch, prototype, and test

- Generate several materially different approaches before converging. The first plausible screen is a hypothesis, not the design.
- Use rough sketches early to explore structure and interaction cheaply. Keep exploratory artifacts visibly unfinished so reviewers critique the idea rather than polish.
- Choose prototype fidelity by the question being tested. Use low fidelity for flow and concepts, and higher fidelity for interaction timing, comprehension, and visual hierarchy.
- Test representative tasks with representative users and realistic data. Avoid explaining the interface before observing where expectations diverge.
- Ask users to think aloud when their reasoning matters, but distinguish what they say from what they actually do.
- Record task success, critical errors, hesitation, workarounds, confidence, and recovery, then connect findings to a specific design decision.
- Iterate in small cycles. A usability test is a learning activity, not a ceremonial sign-off on a finished design.
