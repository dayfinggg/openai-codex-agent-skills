# Emotion and trust

- Evaluate the experience at visceral, behavioral, and reflective levels: immediate impression, effectiveness in use, and the meaning or trust the product leaves afterward.
- Use aesthetics to support hierarchy, confidence, and pleasure, but never let visual novelty weaken legibility, discoverability, accessibility, or control.
- Keep promises, labels, pricing, privacy choices, and progress indicators accurate. Trust lost through a misleading interaction outweighs a short-term conversion gain.
- Design respectful defaults and make consequential choices understandable without coercion, hidden opt-outs, or false urgency.
- Use motion, sound, haptics, and personality to reinforce state and brand only when they remain optional, accessible, and appropriate to context.
