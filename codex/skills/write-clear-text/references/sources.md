# Sources and Provenance

These sources were inspected on 2026-08-17. The skill distills principles rather than copying a single style guide. Language-specific conventions and the user's explicit style always take precedence.

## Model and prompt guidance

1. [OpenAI GPT-5.6 model guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latest-model) recommends defining response content and tone through observable choices, leading with the conclusion, preserving evidence and caveats, and trimming repetition, generic reassurance, and optional background first.
2. [OpenAI skill authoring guide](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/build-skills) supports focused skills, imperative steps, explicit inputs and outputs, progressive disclosure, and concise trigger descriptions.

## Plain language and technical writing

3. [Digital.gov plain-language principles](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/principles) emphasizes audience, logical organization, short and simple expression, active voice, and avoidance of unnecessary jargon.
4. [U.S. National Archives principles of clear writing](https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/write/legal-docs/clear-writing.html) explains how active voice clarifies the actor and recommends direct, strong language and short sentences where appropriate.
5. [GOV.UK clear-language guidance](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/) requires plain English even for specialist audiences and rejects needless official or technical wording.
6. [Google developer documentation style highlights](https://developers.google.com/style/highlights) supports conversational, accessible, consistent, user-focused technical documentation with active voice and second person.
7. [Google guidance for a global audience](https://developers.google.com/style/translation) recommends clear, concise, unambiguous sentences, stable terminology, and avoidance of idioms and culture-bound language.
8. [Microsoft voice guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/brand-voice-above-all-simple-human) recommends putting the key takeaway first, using everyday language, writing for scanning, and pruning excess words.
9. [Microsoft writing style for apps](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/writing-style) applies direct, calm, helpful language to interface copy, errors, actions, accessibility, and localization.
10. [Purdue OWL paragraph organization](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/graduate_writing/introduction_to_writing/documents/drafting-your-document/organization-at-the-paragraph-level.pdf) supports one main topic per paragraph, topic guidance, coherent support, and a clear takeaway.

## Writing traditions

11. [William Strunk, The Elements of Style](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/37134/37134-h/37134-h.htm) provides the durable editing principle that every word should contribute. This skill treats it as a revision test, not a command to make every sentence short or to ban passive voice.
12. [George Orwell, Politics and the English Language](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-%20works/politics-and-the-english-language/) argues for concrete meaning over stale metaphor, pretentious diction, and automatic phrasing. Its rules are applied as heuristics rather than universal grammar.
13. [Maxim Ilyakhov and Lyudmila Sarycheva, Write, Cut](https://bureau.ru/books/pishi/4) presents Russian information-style editing centered on clear thought, reader benefit, structure, and removal of verbal clutter.
14. [Glavred methodology](https://glvrd.ru/about/) identifies stop words, advertising and journalistic clichés, poor syntax, and bureaucratic language. This skill uses those signals diagnostically, not as an automatic word ban.

## Research on LLM prose

15. [Do LLMs write like humans?](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11874169/) reports that instruction-tuned models show a distinct noun-heavy, informationally dense style compared with human writing.
16. [Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016) finds abrupt increases in certain style words after widespread LLM adoption, supporting attention to vocabulary patterns rather than claims about authorship from one word.
17. [Human-LLM Coevolution: Evidence from Academic Writing](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.657/) shows that public awareness can change the frequency of conspicuous words such as “delve,” reinforcing that anti-slop editing must target function and pattern, not a frozen blacklist.
18. [Corpus-driven analysis of ChatGPT-generated academic text](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024384124001694) reports overuse of infrequent academic vocabulary and excessively flowery language in the studied generated corpus.

## Interpretation limits

The research above studies particular models, prompts, languages, domains, and time periods. It does not justify identifying authorship from style alone. The skill uses observed tendencies to improve readability and credibility, never to label a text as machine-generated.
