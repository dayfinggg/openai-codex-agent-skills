# Interface Patterns

Select patterns from the information and task, not from a universal landing-page template.

## Marketing and landing pages

Lead with a concrete product meaning, useful proof, and one primary action. The first viewport may be typographic, product-led, editorial, demonstrative, or image-led. A full-screen photograph is not the default.

Build a narrative through problem, mechanism, proof, use, and action only when those sections are real. Replace fake logo strips, testimonials, awards, metrics, and customer quotes with product demonstrations, screenshots, methods, materials, or clearly marked prototype content.

Use expressive type and motion within the typography limits. Keep paragraphs readable and avoid a giant slogan followed by many interchangeable cards.

## Product and workflow applications

Prioritize task completion, state, speed, and predictable navigation. Use a sidebar only when several persistent destinations justify it. Otherwise consider top navigation, a workspace switcher, command surface, split view, or contextual navigation.

Expose the primary object, current status, next action, filters, and recoverable errors. Support loading, empty, partial, permission-limited, offline, conflict, and success states. Keep frequent actions near the object they affect.

Use compact 14–16px typography, 40–48px controls, clear selection, and restrained motion. Keep the workspace mostly flat. Use dividers and column alignment before rounded panels. Do not apply marketing-scale headings or decorative image heroes to a working tool.

## Data, analytics, and administration

Choose tables for exact comparison, charts for patterns, lists for sequence, and cards for independent summary units. Align numbers, use tabular figures, label units, show time ranges, and make filter state visible.

Do not turn every metric into a card or place four bordered summaries in a rounded strip. Prefer an aligned summary row, typographic hierarchy, a compact definition list, or direct integration with the chart or table. On narrow screens, prioritize, stack, summarize, or offer a deliberate detailed view. Do not merely render a desktop dashboard at a smaller scale.

Use semantic status colors with text or icons. Motion should help track filtering, drill-down, sorting, and state change without replaying every visualization.

## Commerce and catalogs

Let product imagery, names, variants, price, availability, and material information dominate. Product cards are appropriate because items are independent, but keep their chrome quiet. Make filters, sorting, comparison, saved state, and cart feedback clear.

Use honest aspect ratios and consistent image treatment. Avoid cropping away essential product details, invented reviews, false scarcity, fake discounts, and generic lifestyle copy. Preserve filters and scroll state across navigation.

## Editorial, portfolios, and cultural sites

Use strong measure, rhythm, sequence, captions, and art direction. Controlled asymmetry, expressive type, and full-bleed media may be appropriate, but reading and navigation remain stable. Let the work vary the composition rather than repeating one image-left or three-card grid.

Use metadata and project facts at readable sizes. Avoid the automatic formula of dark photography, thin white navigation, huge serif heading, tiny uppercase eyebrow, and orange accent for every “premium” brand.

## Forms, onboarding, and settings

Prefer a clear single-column flow for dependent questions. Place labels above controls, keep helper and error text adjacent, group related fields, and show progress only when it helps. Preserve entered data after errors.

Use multi-step flows for meaningful cognitive boundaries, not to make a short form look sophisticated. Make optional fields explicit, confirm irreversible actions, and provide recovery.

## Mobile web

Prioritize the current task, thumb reach, safe areas, keyboard behavior, and content reflow. Convert wide navigation and dense toolbars into deliberate mobile structures. Avoid hiding essential actions behind unlabeled icons.

Do not make mobile a narrow desktop page. Reconsider hierarchy, controls, filters, tables, charts, dialogs, hover-dependent information, and fixed elements. Test with the virtual keyboard and long text.

## Distinctiveness

Define one product-specific signature move: a meaningful data view, editorial grid, material treatment, typographic voice, interaction, illustration system, or content structure. The rest of the system should support it quietly.

If the design could be relabeled for an unrelated product without changing composition, typography, imagery, or interaction, it is not specific enough.
