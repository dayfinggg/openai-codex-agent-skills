1. Before editing, read producers, consumers, scheduler configuration, message shape, concurrency, and worker lifecycle. Establish client and broker versions, delivery guarantees, acknowledgment mode, retry policy, ordering, and crash behavior. Do not infer exactly-once execution from deduplication or acknowledgment settings.
2. Trace database writes, external effects, enqueueing, and acknowledgment against transaction commit. Reuse an existing transactional outbox or deduplication mechanism. Make business idempotency durable and safe under concurrent duplicates where required. A process-local flag or queue job ID does not protect an external effect after a crash. For a non-idempotent external request with an uncertain result, require supported provider idempotency or reconciliation before retrying.
3. Preserve supported events and ordering. Classify transient and permanent failures, bound attempts, elapsed time, and concurrency within operational limits, and retain poison messages for the established failed-job or dead-letter flow. Avoid immediate requeue loops. Configure actual I/O deadlines and cancellation, not just a timeout that abandons a still-running effect.
4. Stop taking new work during graceful shutdown, drain or cancel active work under the deployment grace period, and close resources after processing settles. Account for crash recovery and redelivery after forced termination. Do not acknowledge before the durable result unless the established contract intentionally does so. Keep scheduler timezone, daylight-saving, missed-run, overlap, and multi-instance behavior when the schedule changes.
5. Consult only the applicable framework notes and verify them against the installed version.
   - [BullMQ](https://docs.bullmq.io/patterns/idempotent-jobs) requires retry-safe final state. Its [shutdown procedure](https://docs.bullmq.io/guide/workers/graceful-shutdown) waits for active TypeScript jobs without a built-in deadline. Match the SDK and deployment deadline rather than assuming close cancels work.
   - [Celery 5.6 tasks](https://docs.celeryq.dev/en/stable/userguide/tasks.html) default to early acknowledgment. Late acknowledgment still has worker-loss exceptions. Inspect `acks_late` and worker-loss behavior rather than toggling them blindly. Its [periodic task guidance](https://docs.celeryq.dev/en/stable/userguide/periodic-tasks.html) requires a single scheduler per schedule and accounts for overlapping runs.
   - [Sidekiq](https://github.com/sidekiq/sidekiq/wiki/Best-Practices) recommends simple JSON arguments and transactional, idempotent jobs. Completed work may run again before acknowledgment. Verify the installed edition's recovery mechanism rather than assuming durability from retry support.
   - [RabbitMQ 4.4 acknowledgments](https://www.rabbitmq.com/docs/confirms) separate publisher confirms from consumer acknowledgments. A confirm does not prove a business transaction committed. Check channel-scoped delivery tags, prefetch, and requeue behavior.
   - [SQS standard queues](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSSimpleQueueService/latest/SQSDeveloperGuide/standard-queues-at-least-once-delivery.html) can redeliver even after deletion. [Visibility timeout](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSSimpleQueueService/latest/SQSDeveloperGuide/sqs-visibility-timeout.html) is not deletion or an exactly-once guarantee. Check renewal, expiry, and deletion against actual processing completion.
6. Verify the normal path, duplicate delivery, partial failure, exhausted retry, and interruption between effect and acknowledgment. Assert durable state and message lifecycle, not only handler return values. Test concurrent duplicates when the change affects deduplication, and timeout or shutdown when their lifecycle changes. For schedules, check the affected overlap or clock boundary. Use isolated queues and dependencies, not live destructive probes. Run the project's language-specific tests and operational diagnostics where applicable.
7. Report preserved or explicitly changed delivery behavior, observed durable effects, retry exhaustion, and interruption recovery. Name any missing broker integration or untested crash window. Reuse the existing framework and broker rather than replacing infrastructure for a local fix.
